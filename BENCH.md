# BENCH.md: every experiment, kept or not

## How to read this

- **Scorer** is the only number that counts: the track page's time for a commit, on the hidden ~20M-event practice set.
- **x86 A/B** is what decides whether a change is kept: the `bench` workflow builds two commits with the Dockerfile and runs them interleaved on one GitHub x86-64 machine (2 cores / 4 threads, stand-in 20M-event dataset from `src/bin/synth.rs`). Times are the in-process total in ms, minimum of 5 runs, base → head. Noise between repeats is about 1%. The CPU differs from run to run, so only compare within a row.
- The workflow's 4-thread number has two threads to a core; its **one-thread** number has a core to itself. They can disagree: fewer instructions pay when threads share a core (experiment 22), a stall avoided pays when they do not (experiment 24). The track page says each run gets "8 pinned vCPUs (hyperthreads)", so the scorer is the first case, 4 cores with two threads each: **the 4-thread number is the one to believe.**
- **bench.sh 20M** is `scripts/bench.sh 20M` on the development Mac (ARM, 8 threads, the organizers' generator, seed 120): best / median wall ms of 5. The Mac is under memory pressure and its numbers wander by 10% or more; they are here because the rules ask for one, not because they decide anything.
- **Verified** means: 36 fixtures, `make check-dev`, `scripts/diff_vs_ref.sh` on seeds 2 to 5, on the native build and on an x86-64 build (`scripts/regress.sh`), then CI on real amd64.

## The probe (branch `probe`)

Not for scoring. It runs the pipeline and then makes `user_daily` empty so that the dev check fails: a failed dev check is the only case in which the track page shows a submission its log. - **the whole parse phase on 1, 2, 4, 6, 8, 12 and 16 threads**, and `read()` + scan alone: whether the 8 CPUs are 8 cores or 4 cores' hyperthreads, so whether the 4-thread or the one-thread column of this file is the one to believe, and whether memory bandwidth caps the scan;

Left: the hash worked out once (`sys::mix` is 2.6% of the samples, `sys.rs:260` and `:261`, and 2.6% of the instructions: a line's person and event are hashed in parse, then again in dedup and in the people phase, which a key stored already hashed, by a hash that can be undone, would not need); the `ts` flags; field bounds read from memory instead of extracted from a vector register. **`black_box` in a register** ("What 36 says"): the flag `parse` branches on goes through the stack, a store, a `lea` and a reload a line and a few cycles more before a wrong guess is found out; an `asm!` block with `inout(reg)` hides it as well. About 3 instructions a line and a little of each misprediction: 0.2 to 0.4% by estimate, an hour's work with the assembly read before and after (the compiler must still not split the branch: callgrind's count of mispredictions on that line says whether it did). **The reads of a `Vec`'s pointer**: `alloc/src/raw_vec/mod.rs:635` is `RawVec::ptr`, 2.24% of the samples and 1.59% of the instructions (17.8 million for 1.30 million lines, about 14 reads a line). Which `Vec`s has not been found (`ReadBuf::bytes`, `Combine::chunks`, the catalog's `slots` and `by_number` are the ones the line loop reaches); a raw pointer taken once outside the loop would not be read again. Each is one load that hits the first-level cache, so expect less than its share of the samples.
11. **The people phase's grouping**: the staging of events (`person.rs:264`) 0.92%, their scatter to their people (`person.rs:276` and `:277`) 0.75% (1.1% and 1.8% in cycle 10's profiles), the hash probes that number the people (`person.rs:128`, `:152`) about 0.9%. A partition's working set there is about 850 KB (records, two staging arrays, the result; worked out from the record counts, not measured), more than a Zen 3 core's 512 KB second-level cache; partition counts were swept and made no difference (fewer partitions make parse faster and people slower by as much). Its small arrays (the people's numbers, cursors and starts, under 32 KB each) are the system allocator's, on 4 KB pages, about 20 of them a thread: by the reading of 32 that is too few to matter, and it is not measured. No design; 26 showed that an extra pass costs more than a sort.
12. **Smaller output.** `user_daily` is 78 MB of PLAIN Parquet at 20M events. Dictionary and run-length encodings would cut it to about a half without touching `revenue_cents` (a person's rows follow one another, so `user_id` is one run a person; `day` has 14 values; `sessions` and `events` mostly fit a byte): worked out on paper, nothing built. The kernel's side of the write was 0.26% of samples on Sapphire Rapids and 0.2 to 0.5% on the AMD runners: worth little inside the process. Outside it, GitHub's Docker took about 35 ms longer to end a container that had written 222 MB to disk than one that wrote to memory (experiment 17, 60M), and 10 to 23 ms longer for 78 MB in one of the two 20M measurements, nothing in the other; whether the scorer's sandbox does is one more thing only the probe or a score can say, and every encoding other than PLAIN is one more thing the checker's reader has to get right.

The wide loop already does it (the table is `DELIMITER_BY_LOW_NIBBLE` in `src/scan.rs`).
